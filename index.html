          Buffers

          Buffers the IR allocates (memref.alloc, memref.alloca, gpu.alloc), baseline against current. Each bar is a buffer's live range through its function, top to bottom in the source: from the allocation to its dealloc, to the end when it is returned (marked edge), or else to its last use. A use inside a loop keeps it live until the loop ends. Sizes are static; buffers with dynamic shapes are counted but not sized. Peak live is the largest function's. Click a current buffer to show its line.

          No memref allocations. Buffers appear after bufferization, for example -one-shot-bufferize.

          Symbol history

          Every function, kernel, GPU module and global in the trace, with the passes that created it, changed its body, lowered it to another op (func.func → llvm.func), or removed it. Nested symbols are named as in gpu.launch_func (@module::@kernel), and a change inside a nested symbol is not counted against its parent. Click a pass to go to it.

            VizMLIR / field guide

            See what your MLIR pass changed.

            VizMLIR turns two MLIR snapshots into a graph and a focused structural diff. Everything runs in your browser; your IR stays local.

            Start here

            • Open Workspace and paste the earlier IR into Baseline.
            • Paste the lowered or transformed IR into Current.
            • Use the graph to inspect operation relationships and the pass diff to find added, removed, and changed operations.

            Step through a pass pipeline

            Capture every pass boundary from mlir-opt and open the log with Open…, or paste it into Current:

            mlir-opt input.mlir -pass-pipeline='…' -mlir-print-ir-after-all -mlir-print-ir-module-scope -mlir-disable-threading 2> trace.txt

            Pick a pass from the timeline at the top (or press [ / ]) to compare its IR with the IR it received. Passes that failed are marked with ✗, and compiler errors, warnings, notes, and remarks printed around a dump appear over the graph. Without -mlir-print-ir-module-scope, nested passes print only the function they ran on; VizMLIR rebuilds module baselines from those dumps.

            Add -mlir-timing to see how long each pass took: the timeline shows each pass's wall time and IR size, and p opens the full report. mlir-opt does not measure memory per pass, so for peak memory run it under /usr/bin/time -l (macOS) or /usr/bin/time -v (Linux) and keep that output in the log.

            Try a small example

            This example creates a tensor, fills it, and multiplies it. The graph turns each operation into a node and draws edges for SSA data flow.

            Then change linalg.fill and compare.

            Keep it live, save it, share it

            • Watch… (Chrome and Edge) reloads a file every time it changes. Point it at trace.txt, re-run mlir-opt in your terminal, and VizMLIR updates on the same pass.
            • Your workspace autosaves in this browser. Sessions keeps named snapshots and imports or downloads them as .json files. Nothing is uploaded.
            • Export the whole graph as PNG or SVG from the toolbar over the graph, and the diff as Markdown or JSON from the diff pane. Pick changes with x or shift/⌘-click and press c to copy them as a patch snippet.

            How to read the visual

            Follow the arrows from values being produced to operations that consume them. Click a node to see its label and parent. The pass diff on the right highlights what was added, removed, or changed between the two snapshots.
